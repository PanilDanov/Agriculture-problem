## Collect local inputs for any field

Save the field polygon, WGS84 longitude and latitude, the relevant day convention or IANA time zone, units, provider and dataset version, observation or issue time, and provenance for each input. Use measured values when available. Label gridded, extrapolated, modeled, and farmer-reported values with their origin. Record missing observations as missing. [Copernicus dataset documentation](https://cds.climate.copernicus.eu/datasets/sis-agrometeorological-indicators?tab=overview) · [SoilGrids documentation](https://docs.isric.org/globaldata/soilgrids/SoilGrids_faqs_01.html)

In the FAO Crop Calendar UI select country, crop, and the closest matching agroecological zone. Record the calendar's location, cropping system, and dates. In the alternative API use Swagger to list countries and select the country crop-calendar endpoint. Some crop and zone combinations have no entry. ## Water calculations and units

1. Determine four local stages from crop observations or a calibrated calendar. Use Kc initial throughout the initial stage and Kc mid throughout mid-season. Interpolate linearly from initial to mid during development, and from mid to end in the late stage. Compute **standard crop water demand** as `ETc = Kc × ET₀` in mm/day for each day. Standard demand presumes a well-managed crop with adequate water. Cumulative standard demand is the sum across days with coverage. [FAO Chapter 6, constructing the crop coefficient curve](https://agrhysmo.agr.unipi.it/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/FAO56%202025.pdf#page=234)
2. For each active soil layer calculate `1000 × (theta_fc − theta_wp) × layer_thickness_m` in mm. Sum across layers for total available water, TAW. Adjust the tabular depletion fraction with `p = clamp(p_ref + 0.04 × (5 − ETc), 0.1, 0.8)`, then compute readily available water as `RAW = p × TAW`. The FAO p tables describe relatively long intervals typical of surface irrigation. Use a locally supported adjustment for other wetting regimes. [FAO Chapter 8, Equations 8.3–8.5](https://agrhysmo.agr.unipi.it/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/FAO56%202025.pdf#page=294)
3. As a snapshot, calculate depletion from field moisture in each layer using `1000 × (theta_fc − theta_now) × layer_thickness_m`. Limit each contribution to the interval from zero to its available storage. The CLI displays whether estimated total depletion has reached RAW and the theoretical storage deficit to field capacity. If the field application efficiency is known, divide the deficit by that efficiency for the corresponding gross depth. Convert a depth in mm to a whole-field volume using `mm × hectares × 10` m³. Set application based on forecast rain, infiltration, runoff, method, management targets, and allocation. A seasonal water budget uses a daily balance across the season and accounts for effective rainfall, irrigations, and soil water carry-over. [FAO Chapter 8](https://agrhysmo.agr.unipi.it/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/FAO56%202025.pdf#page=294)

## Crop rotation evidence

The nine checks in the catalog cover all ten crops. Each one links to the agency and section used to derive it. The program reports matching hosts and elapsed time since their last recorded harvest. A match calls for local review, since the applicable pathogen, host susceptibility, climate, residue, and cropping system can change the advice. A field history with no match remains subject to local crop, disease, and pesticide-label review.

| Crop | Evidence entered and how to apply it |
| --- | --- |
| Wheat | Wheat and barley can share Fusarium crown rot. Victorian guidance describes how a two-year break with a locally suitable non-susceptible crop or fallow can reduce crown rot following wheat or barley, and points to volunteer cereals and weeds as possible hosts. Susceptible wheat and chickpea can also share root-lesion nematodes. Check species and cultivars before recommending that break. ## Integration and verification

The inputs are location-neutral. Numerical results require the local field inputs listed above, and applicability of rotation intervals requires regional evidence. The assessment CLI works offline with supplied inputs and returns reference crop demand, a soil-depletion snapshot when the field data suffice, and source-linked rotation matches. Its fixture is a labeled synthetic example. Geographic data acquisition and a seasonal irrigation budget require the data collection workflow above. [CLI instructions and data contract](README.md)

Verification reconciled all 24 coefficient, height, and rooting-depth rows and all ten p values against the cited FAO pages. Seven agronomy tests cover every crop variant, the cross-year example, variant and coefficient selection, shared hosts, and incomplete or invalid local inputs. The nine yield tests also passed in Python 3.11. Both English workbooks were reopened and checked against their translated data matrices and rendered for review.

The frontend analysis request includes the field boundary, available soil pH and texture, irrigation method and allocation when known, and a single previous crop ID. The analysis API now combines the field collector and crop profiles to choose two-season schedules and recommend sowing days where local calendar evidence supports them. A field irrigation schedule still requires locally sourced phenology, the selected irrigation variant, and water observations. [Two-season field analysis API](analysis-api.md)
